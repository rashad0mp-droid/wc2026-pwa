# Treatment — "105 Minutes of Knocking"

Style: **Sports Telestrator** (Lemo-Opuscar). 1920×1080, 30 fps, English voice-over, burned-in subtitles.
Topic: the 2026 World Cup final, Spain 1–0 Argentina (a.e.t.), 19 July 2026, MetLife Stadium. Facts and sources: `FACTS.md`.

## Brief (as agreed)

- About 45 s, English pundit narrator (Kokoro TTS), burned-in subtitles plus a `.srt` file.
- **No match footage.** Every frame of the final is owned by the rights holder, and no openly licensed clip exists. The user chose to keep the telestrator show (freezes, rings, marker arrows, score bar, rewind, flatten) and play it on a **code-drawn broadcast-angle pitch** reconstructed from match reports. The film says so on every analysis shot (a `RECONSTRUCTION` tag where the source clock would sit). Player positions are illustrative, so the film states no distances or times measured from them. Every number on screen comes from the published match statistics.
- Fictional show name: **CHALK TALK**. No tournament logos, trophy, kits replicas, broadcaster or sponsor marks. The teams appear only as national names and plain kit colours (Spain red/navy, Argentina sky-blue/white stripes).

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Chronological build-up**: a light freeze on Spain's 93' header (saved), then the full rewind-and-freeze breakdown of the 106' goal, then the flatten to a board that sums up the match.
2. **One moment from three angles**: only the goal, from a main camera, a reverse angle and a top-down view. This shows the goal but loses the story of the dominance before it.
3. **"What went wrong" for Argentina**: a defensive breakdown ending on a space map. The space map would require positional claims that the sources don't support.

**Chosen: 1.** The story is pressure, then release. Spain knock for 105 minutes, the keeper keeps saying no, and two substitutes finally open it up. Structure 1 gives the secondary chance a light treatment and keeps the deep breakdown for the goal, which is how the style wants it. Opening: the strike frame frozen in the first second, then a rewind (a hook). Ending: the pitch flattens into the chalk board and the data card, with one number circled.

## 2. Logline and arc

*Spain had 65% of the ball and twelve shots on target, but no goal until two substitutes combined in extra time.*
Setup: the goal frozen, then rewound ("no goals, then this"). Turn: 93', a header saved; the pressure keeps failing. Ending: the 106' goal taken apart freeze by freeze, the score flips, the pitch flattens into the record. Argentina's zero shots in 90 minutes is circled.

## 3. Benchmark

Televised touch-screen analysis segments in football, and match-analysis explainer channels.
- **Learn:** watch first, explain second; one finding per freeze; marker lines that obey the pitch's perspective; packaging that stays still while the picture moves.
- **Don't take:** any broadcaster's logos, idents, fonts, colours or show names; any real footage or stills.

## 6. Cue map

120 BPM (0.5 s per beat). The groove restarts at the head of each played segment. On every freeze the drums stop: brass stab + timpani + low thump + shutter, then a closed hi-hat only. On the goal frame (≈33.0): big brass chord, crash and glockenspiel on the score flip. Under the flatten: a long whoosh and a timpani roll; then the groove returns, half-time, under the data card and resolves on the last line.
Instruments: drum kit, electric bass, staccato trumpet and trombone, timpani, horn, glockenspiel (the style's sampled band).
